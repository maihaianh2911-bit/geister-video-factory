# Lessons (bài học của kênh)

Canonical visual rules: [scene_system.md — Visual direction](scene_system.md#visual-direction-canonical-quality-rules).
This file records past mistakes and their prevention. Do not duplicate the full brief or introduce competing timing/style rules. New owner feedback: merge into the matching lesson; add a new line only for a distinct failure. Keep this list around 40 lines or fewer.

- Grip and orientation: fingers wrap the prop; it follows the hand until released (v13: bottle upside down). It may tilt when the action calls for it. A tool that moves (shovel, detector, pen, magnifier, thumb on a phone) is always drawn in a hand connected to an arm, even in a close-up (v63); an arm or hand the same colour as the body behind it gets an outline, skin or cuff so the grip reads, and an object passed between people always sits in a hand — giver → both hands → receiver, never a jump. Draw a held prop from the hand's ACTUAL reached position (arm length clamps the target), never from the hand target, so set-down/pick-up read as hand+prop to contact → release, or reach → grip → lift (v77).
- Show the action the script describes. A closed door is not a substitute for a visible person's reaction. Expressions match the event, rather than one fixed expression for all scenes.
- Preserve identity across shots and close-ups: silhouette, proportions, hair, clothes, accessories; arms connect through shoulders/wrists with natural lengths.
- Check support and contact in motion: feet on the floor, body on the seat, hands on their target; no floating props or limbs crossing through objects. A fall or crowd collapse is a chain of contacts (push → hands on the next back → knees buckle → hands/knees on the ground → the next person over the fallen body) drawn with bending knees/elbows, never a rigid figure rotating and sliding (v73). A verb done to an object (pry, push, lift) puts the hands on that object with fixed arm lengths; lift a person under the arms/torso, never by the head, and keep a set's levels (e.g. a higher floor) identical across shots (v69).
- Keep the subtitle area readable on every background; light/busy scenes need the subtitle box (v31: white text vanished on cream and over a basket).
- Time counters to the spoken word; compute related values together so they agree (v30: fold count/thickness; v31: area). A timing.json with `precision: estimated` or runs of identical word lengths is an even split, not real speech: realign it on the existing voice before timing anything to words (v73).
- Do not invent factual names, dates or quantities (v31: invented towns), nor assign a generic fact to the viewer's own object (v70: which panes of "your car" are ESG). Neutral set dressing is allowed when it does not imply an unsupported fact. Visual symbols keep the script's strength: 'very hard/partly/rarely' gets a strain, slowdown or high gauge, never a ban sign, lock or cross-out, which mean 'impossible/never' (v76).
- Story state never resets: once a character has escaped/finished, a later conditional line ("as long as someone is still trapped") is shown as a clearly separate rule card/pictogram, not by putting that character back (v70).
- Cards, boards and screens appear with their content; no empty containers or repeated tableau carrying unrelated lines (v42).
- Frame zero must reveal the topic at phone size; a dark scene still needs a clear focal point — in night videos every shot keeps a lit, phone-sized subject (a person doing the action, not abstract dots or arcs), and no shot breaks the video's tone with a full-frame flat colour. Repeated title cards (e.g. REGEL n) share one size, position and backdrop style (v60). Use the canonical opening rules instead of a decorative flash/zoom.
- Use the canonical pacing targets; compare progression rather than merely counting cuts. Repeated crops, dust or idle loops do not fix a static scene.
- Match the chosen style within the video. Lottie/3D or engine helpers must fit its palette and proportions; do not assemble mismatched clip-art.
- Serious, clear cause and effect for these channels; no silly gags or comic boing/womp effects.
- Before delivery, inspect the actual final video. Fix clipped/overlapping text, unreadable subtitles, unexplained blank/static sections, distorted/changing bodies, mismatched assets, poor subject contrast, incorrect facts and visuals out of sync with the voice. Use short motion segments for contact/timing problems; stills alone can miss them.
- Record only inspections actually performed. Technical checks do not judge beauty or motion. Use the existing per-scene review; the finished video is reviewed by ChatGPT, whose fix requests arrive as owner comments on the issue.
- Asynchronous loading (fonts, data) takes its `delayRender()` handle inside a component, never at module load: a module-level handle is orphaned when Remotion re-initialises and times out mid-render although the load finished.
- Walking on stairs: each foot lands on a tread and the body drops after it; a figure translated diagonally reads as sliding. Dates/places go into a scene with action, not a row of separate cards, and on-screen text never says more than the voice (v63).
- A shot never resolves a problem with an object or option the narration has just ruled out ("only a phone" → no cash in hand); show the open problem and keep the solution for the shot that names it. When contrasting "offline" with "still works", cross out only the connection/online service, never the whole device that still runs (v75).
